Convenience Stores

Independent c-stores across the Fraser Valley — tobacco, lottery, and lease-based businesses with their own due diligence considerations.

Text MananGet In Touch

Mostly Leasehold Businesses, With Their Own Financial Rhythm

Most convenience stores in the Fraser Valley are leasehold businesses rather than real estate sales, which makes lease terms often the single most important piece of due diligence in a transaction.

Tobacco and lottery revenue carry structurally different, more predictable margins than general merchandise — understanding the mix is key to accurately valuing the business.

How Convenience Stores Are Valued

Key drivers of value in this category

Lease Terms Remaining term, renewal options, and rent escalation clauses, since most c-stores are leasehold businesses.
Tobacco & Lottery Mix These categories carry predictable, verifiable margins distinct from general merchandise revenue.
Sales Verification Cross-checked against supplier invoices and BCLC lottery statements for a realistic picture.
Location & Traffic Foot and vehicle traffic patterns specific to convenience retail, not general retail assumptions.

Convenience Stores Due Diligence

What to verify before you commit

Licensing Review Tobacco retail licence, lottery terminal agreement, and any liquor licensing tied to the business.
Lease Analysis Term, renewal options, and rent escalation — the top due diligence priority for a leasehold business.
Sales Cross-Verification Comparing reported sales against supplier invoices and BCLC lottery commission statements.
Inventory & Fixtures Assessing current inventory value and condition of coolers, shelving, and POS equipment.
Landlord Relationship Understanding the landlord's history with the tenant and any pending disputes or rent issues.
Competitive Landscape Nearby convenience and grocery competition that could affect future sales.

Convenience Stores Questions, Answered

Most commercial leases include an assignment clause requiring landlord consent — confirm this early, since a landlord can decline or add conditions.

Once financials are verified and lease assignment is confirmed, these transactions typically move faster than real estate deals — often four to eight weeks to close.
